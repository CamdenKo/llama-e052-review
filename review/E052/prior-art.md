## Exact contract boundary

At that base, `ggml/include/ggml.h:2684-2701` specifies lightning-indexer shapes, prescaled weights, F16 masks and broadcasting. It does not specify exceptional score behavior or require masked NaN propagation. The current native CPU implementation computes a score and adds the mask (`ops.cpp:12490-12508`); native Metal does the same after half staging (`fa_aux.metal:423-479`). Those implementations establish observable arithmetic behavior, including possible masked NaN. Absence of an explicit exceptional contract does not make every behavior change invisible.

[Header](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/dde879bc4f954b809892fd0d660515f42536394a/ggml/include/ggml.h#L2684-L2701), [CPU arithmetic](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/dde879bc4f954b809892fd0d660515f42536394a/ggml/src/ggml-cpu/ops.cpp#L12490-L12508), [Metal half staging and epilogue](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/dde879bc4f954b809892fd0d660515f42536394a/ggml/src/ggml-metal/kernels/fa_aux.metal#L423-L479).

Original operator discussion [#24231](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/24231) explains fusion of dot/ReLU/weight reduction and proposes the mask to avoid a separate add. A [maintainer reply](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/24231#issuecomment-4890961350) favors masks for consistency with FlashAttention and discusses F32 compute/possible later precision controls. The checked discussion does not establish a promise to propagate exceptional masked scores. Nor does it explicitly approve this new Metal behavior.

**Source inference:** a narrowly described Metal exclusion convention is defensible as a candidate, with exceptional cross-backend differences made explicit. No precise written upstream contract was found that forces CPU/portable-contract expansion before testing it. Acceptance remains a maintainer judgment. Do not call it arithmetic equivalence or a portable strict-mask guarantee. Finite FP32 q/K can overflow Metal half staging, so finite-input assertions must be backed by a failing-before case.

**Recommendation:** continue the isolated Metal-only candidate. Keep ordinary finite-score CPU/Metal comparisons and unchanged tolerances; evaluate stronger exceptional exclusion with an explicit Metal expected-output oracle. Do not force unchanged CPU to meet that oracle. Broad backend parity remains an optional future proposal, not a demonstrated prerequisite. Human review must assess this disclosed local numerical convention; source precedent does not pre-approve it.
